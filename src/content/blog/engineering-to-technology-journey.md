## The Spark: Why I Started Learning Code

After six years in fire protection engineering, I began to see patterns everywhere — not just in fire systems, but in the documentation, reporting, and compliance workflows that surrounded them. Spreadsheets that should have been databases. Paper forms that should have been apps. Inspection reports that took hours to compile when they could take seconds.

I realized that the bottlenecks in our industry weren't just technical — they were technological. And I wanted to be part of the solution.

## Embracing the Beginner's Mindset

Starting from scratch in software development after years of engineering expertise was humbling. In engineering, I was the expert. In coding, I was back to square one — struggling with syntax errors, wrestling with package managers, and spending hours debugging a missing semicolon.

### What Surprised Me
- The learning curve is steeper than expected, but the compounding effect is real
- The developer community is incredibly generous with knowledge (Stack Overflow, GitHub, open source)
- Engineering thinking transfers surprisingly well: systematic problem-solving, attention to detail, and documentation habits

## The Tech Stack I Chose

I decided to focus on the modern web development stack:

- **Next.js** — For its full-stack capabilities and excellent developer experience
- **TypeScript** — Coming from an engineering background, type safety felt natural
- **Tailwind CSS** — Utility-first styling that maps well to engineering thinking
- **Supabase** — Familiar database concepts with modern API layers

## Bridging Two Worlds

The more I learned, the more I saw connections between fire protection engineering and software development:

| Engineering | Software |
|-------------|----------|
| System design | Software architecture |
| Code compliance | Best practices |
| Risk assessment | Error handling |
| Inspection checklists | Test suites |
| Maintenance schedules | CI/CD pipelines |

Both disciplines are about designing systems that work reliably under constraints. Both require anticipating failure modes before they happen. Both reward methodical thinking over rushed solutions.

## What's Next

I'm currently building my portfolio platform (the site you're reading this on) and exploring ideas for SaaS products at the intersection of engineering and technology. The goal is to create tools that make fire protection engineering workflows faster, more accurate, and more collaborative.

The journey from engineer to technology builder is just beginning.
